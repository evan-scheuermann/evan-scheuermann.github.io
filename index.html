About Me

I'm a mathematics major with a minor in economics at St. John Fisher University, where I maintain a 4.0 GPA and focus on applied quantitative methods. My coursework spans machine learning, econometrics, dynamical systems, linear algebra, and differential equations.

My projects reflect an interest in the intersection of rigorous statistical methodology and practical business problems — from predicting customer churn for banks to quantifying the economic impact of steam power during the Industrial Revolution.

I work primarily in R and Python, with hands-on experience building end-to-end ML pipelines using caret, tidymodels, and ggplot2.

Individual project comparing Elastic Net and Random Forest regression on the Ames housing dataset (~2,930 observations, 82 variables). Econometrics R · ggplot2 Fixed Effects ECON 314

Steam Power & Manufacturing Productivity, 1850

Log-linear regression with state and industry fixed effects applied to 124,860 firms from the 1850 Census of Manufactures. Quantified the productivity premium of steam adoption and its heterogeneous effects across industries. Produced a choropleth heatmap of state fixed-effect coefficients revealing the outsized economic impact of the California Gold Rush.

+45.2% Steam Premium
51.6% R²
